# Literature

One paragraph per curated paper. PDFs in `../pdfs/`.

### [actAsARealResearcher.md](actAsARealResearcher.md) — AARRI-Bench
82 tasks spanning the research lifecycle, run through the Harbor framework across 16 harness–model combinations. The best configuration (Mini-SWE-Agent + Claude Opus 4.7) reaches only **68.3%**, ahead of Hermes (64.6%) and Claude Code (62.2%) — minimalist harnesses scale better with frontier models than heavy scaffolding does. The recurring qualitative finding is that agents "frequently overlook subtle yet critical details that are obvious to real human researchers": in case 26fb63 a manuscript contained fabricated data (every trailing decimal digit identical) and only one of the sixteen configurations noticed, while the rest flagged minor reproducibility issues instead.

### [ambig_ds.md](ambig_ds.md) — Ambig-DS
The most on-point paper for task interpretation. It benchmarks *task-framing* ambiguity — underspecified prediction target (on DSBench) and underspecified objective/metric (on MLE-bench) — and states the thesis directly: "recognizing target and objective underspecification, not pipeline execution, is the bottleneck missing from standard DS-agent evaluations." Frontier backbones submit a plausible alternative framing on **39–63%** of ambiguous runs without flagging it, and user-overridable flagging in the final answer **never exceeds 4%**. Performance drops Δ −0.10 to −0.29 (Target) and −0.04 to −0.09 (Objective), all bootstrap CIs excluding zero. An Ask policy recovers much of the loss (+0.20 Target, +0.09 Objective) but "kills misframing, not abdication" — degenerate, non-committal submissions persist, and a residual Target gap survives even after framing is resolved. Calibration is the unsolved part: every model–policy pair is miscalibrated on at least one suite, with permissive prompts causing over-asking (88% of one model's Target asks were style preferences) and conservative prompts causing silent defaulting on up to **100%** of ambiguous metric runs.

### [autoResearch.md](autoResearch.md) — AutoResearch survey
A survey organising research automation into five workflow conditions (literature grounding, hypothesis formation/planning, experimentation and tool use, feedback/validation/review, reporting) and a five-level autonomy spectrum L0–L4, where L1–L2 is "Vibe Research" and L3–L4 is the frontier. Its per-stage bottleneck claims are sharper than the stage names suggest: grounding fails at "evidential state construction, not retrieval coverage"; planning at "disciplined scientific search and selection pressure, not idea volume"; validation at "robust internal and external filtering, not scoring per se". The central structural finding is that **"reflexive iteration — continuous revision of hypotheses/methods in light of results — is largely absent"**: even AI Scientist-v2's tree search optimises within a fixed research direction, making pipeline-structured systems "good at completing papers but not at conducting research". Evaluation should span novelty, validity, impact, reliability, and provenance, and the recurring evaluation error is **"authority borrowing"** — inferring strength on one dimension from another. No domain yet shows convincing evidence of robust L3 or L4 autonomy.

### [bioAgentBench.md](bioAgentBench.md) — BioAgent Bench
Ten curated end-to-end bioinformatics pipelines (RNA-seq, variant calling, metagenomics, scRNA-seq, comparative genomics), each a natural-language prompt plus input/reference data with a concrete CSV/TSV ground-truth artifact, deliberately curated to sit "closer to software engineering benchmarks rather than biology data analysis benchmarks". Frontier agents complete them almost perfectly — Claude Opus 4.5 100%, Gemini 3 Pro 96.6%, GPT-5.2 and Sonnet 4.5 92.5% — with no custom scaffolding, while the best open-weight model reaches 82.5%. Explicit plan quality correlates with completion at only R = 0.61, and some models complete pipelines despite weak plans. The value is in what completion hides: across four trials of the same task the mean Jaccard overlap of final results is **0.43** (Pearson 0.73), attributed partly to genuine analytic degrees of freedom; corrupted inputs were detected in only 7/10 tasks and in several cases the agent flagged the corruption and continued anyway; decoy files were swallowed by filename globbing and default-database selection; and prompt bloat cost 28% of completed steps. The conclusion — "correct higher-level pipeline construction does not imply correct step-level reasoning" — reframes the question from *does it produce a result* to *can it reliably detect when it should not proceed*.

### [CLAMBER.md](CLAMBER.md)
A ~12K-instance benchmark of ambiguous information needs, organised as three dimensions (Epistemic Misalignment, Linguistic Ambiguity, Aleatoric Output) over eight categories. The headline is a ceiling: the best model reaches only **54.25% accuracy / 52.77% F1** at identifying whether a query is ambiguous at all. Chain-of-thought and few-shot prompting "do not reliably help, and can induce overconfidence" (Llama2-13B's calibration error rises 16.66 points with CoT). The Contradiction category is worst — 38.00 accuracy, 28.17 F1 — with **81.97% of errors being false negatives**, attributed to SFT/RLHF biasing models toward always answering. Clarifying-question quality is separately poor, with "Wrong Aspect" accounting for 52.25% of errors on Aleatoric Output.

### [Gulati_2026_askEarlyAskLateAskRight.md](Gulati_2026_askEarlyAskLateAskRight.md)
Measures *when* clarification is worth asking, by force-injecting the missing information at controlled points along an agent's trajectory. The value-of-information curves are dimension-dependent: goal clarification injected at 10% of the trajectory reaches pass@3 0.78 against an oracle's 0.80 and a no-clarification baseline of 0.40, but decays to baseline by 70% — whereas input-level clarification retains value through roughly the halfway mark. Wasted compute rises monotonically with delay (0.0% → 21.7% on TheAgentCompany; 38.4% → 52.9% on MCP-Atlas). A natural-ask overlay shows agents ask far too late when they ask at all: GPT-5.2 in 52% of sessions at a mean 43% through the trajectory, Claude Sonnet 4.5 in 23% at 50%, Gemini 3 Flash never. The paper's stated limitation is the opening for anything upstream of it: it "does not address the supply side: making agents recognize ambiguity and ask at the right moment."

### [kirgis_CanAIAgentsConductOpenEndedAIresearch.md](kirgis_CanAIAgentsConductOpenEndedAIresearch.md)
Two shadow evaluations in which Opus 4.8 on OpenClaw was given six days and thousands of dollars to reproduce the research behind unpublished NeurIPS 2026 submissions; both outputs were rejected by expert reviewers (2/6 and 1/6). Engineering was not the problem — the agents ran hundreds of experiments and compiled camera-ready LaTeX unaided. Five failure modes recur: poor judgment about the bar for publishable research, uncreative responses to design shortcomings, ineffective backtracking, poor resource awareness, and instruction drift. The concrete instances are the argument: one agent planned 36–48h across three methods and abandoned two after five hours of promising results on the first; the other falsified six approaches in the first 14 hours with **110 hours still remaining** and reframed the goal (arguing no such detector could exist) rather than restarting; both runs ended with less than half the API budget spent; one declared completion seven hours early after a reject verdict. Agents backtracked locally but **never at the project level**, despite freely spawning clean-context subagents for other purposes, and answered uniformly negative self-reviews (0 acceptances across 4 and 10 rounds) by narrowing claims and adding caveats while overweighting the most lenient external reviewer.

### [manyAIanalysistOneDataset.md](manyAIanalysistOneDataset.md)
A ~5,000-run agentic replication of the many-analysts design: 3 datasets × 4 LLMs × 5 personas, of which 3,303 (67%) passed an auditor compliance screen. Given identical data, an identical hypothesis, and a **prespecified estimand**, the runs still disperse in effect size, p-value, and even the **sign** of the estimate. Worse, conclusions are steerable by framing alone: the support-rate gap between a "negative" and a "strong support" persona ranges from 34 to 66 percentage points, consistently across all four models, and compliance filtering narrows but does not eliminate the spread. The recommendations are multiverse-style reporting and disclosing prompts "on par with code and data"; the framework can also be pointed at a published specification to quantify the residual dispersion its undocumented choices leave open.

### [scHeurekaBench.md](scHeurekaBench.md) — HeurekaBench / sc-HeurekaBench
A framework for *constructing* benchmarks of **open-ended, data-driven research questions**, instantiated in single-cell biology as sc-HeurekaBench (50 OEQs + 50 MCQs from 41 validated insights across 13 *Nature*/*Cell* papers). Its defining move is grounding question construction in the scientific process: a multi-LLM pipeline extracts candidate insights from a paper, matches them to scripts in the paper's own repository, and composes a workflow — and an insight is kept **only if human reviewers can run that workflow and reproduce the published result**. Questions are then filtered by having GPT-4o and Claude-4-Sonnet answer them, discarding whatever both get right, to strip out anything answerable from pretraining. The prompts are deliberately non-prescriptive (*"What changes in cytokine expression are observed in the aging muscle microenvironment?"*), explicitly rejecting the single-computational-question shape of BixBench and ScienceAgentBench: the agent must plan the analyses itself. Scoring is an atomic-fact G-Eval where a ground-truth fact counts as PRESENT **only if tied to dataset-derived numbers or cluster identifiers** — correct biology supported by recall or hedged language is downgraded, which is how the rubric penalises answering from memory; non-contradictory novel findings are not penalised. The judge is validated against 11 human experts (Spearman 0.93, κ 0.85). Results are uniformly low: the best configuration reaches **2.58/5** (Claude-4-Sonnet as Biomni's planner), the best open-weight 2.08, and a bare Claude-4-Sonnet without an agent loop 1.90. The sharpest design finding is that **critic placement inverts the effect** — a critic at the *end* of the loop lifts GPT-OSS-120B from 2.04 to 2.49, while the same critic on the *initial plan* costs 0.13–0.19 points by disrupting reasoning trajectories. Named failure modes: recalling canonical markers instead of running enrichment, never exploring the metadata columns, and using literature to eliminate MCQ options "which actively goes against the idea of data-driven discovery".

### [Su_2026_knowingButNotShowing.md](Su_2026_knowingButNotShowing.md)
Separates *recognising* ambiguity from *acting* on it, on 1,000 AmbigQA items across ten models. Asked to judge ambiguity explicitly, models are right on the ambiguous class 60–80% of the time; asked to answer the same questions, they give a direct answer more than 95% of the time and are judged "only answered" on 80–95%, with clarification rare even for the best family (~5% for Claude). Retrieved context makes it worse on the axis that matters: it raises QA accuracy but further suppresses clarification and pushes models toward the "unambiguous" label, suggesting they read the presence of passages as a cue that the question is answerable rather than reasoning about whether it still admits multiple readings. Mapping model explanations onto a six-type taxonomy, **Scope ambiguities are strongly under-assigned** relative to human annotators while Identity and Version are over-assigned. The diagnosis is a reward-misalignment one: models know, and are not trained to show.
